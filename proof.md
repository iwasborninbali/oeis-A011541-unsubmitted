# Arithmetic of the seventh bound

Let A=24153319581254312065344 and B=24885189317885898975235988544, the listed sixth term and seventh upper bound. Direct multiplication gives

A=2^6·3^3·7^4·13·19·43·73·79^3·97·157 and B=101^3 A.

All the displayed bases are prime, distinct, and different from 101; these facts can be checked by trial division up to their square roots. Therefore ω(A)=10 and ω(B)=11, where ω counts distinct prime factors. If a(7)=B, then ω(a(7))>ω(a(6)) and ω(a(7))≤14. This establishes both stated conditions at the transition to n=7; it does not decide any later transition or the conjecture as a whole. It takes the listed value of a(6) as given.
